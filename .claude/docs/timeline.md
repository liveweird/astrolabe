### The shared Timeline component (binding reference; built v0.3.0, extended through 1.4.0)

One component serves every Gantt-like page: the project view (v0.3, milestones of one project), the team view (v0.5, milestones against capacity), the roadmap and the programs view (v0.6). Pages differ in their rows and bar sources,
not in the mechanics. **The component draws and proposes; it never writes and never decides** — the page adapts its view document to rows (`TimelineRowData[]`), reads the `can:{…}` flags for `editable`, and answers a proposed change with its own write path
(optimistic update, the server's verdict, rollback — `hooks/useRoadmapRanking.ts` is the template). The server alone decides who may schedule (`PUT /milestones/{id}/schedule`, team manager or ADMIN).

Files (all flat under `web/src/`): `utils/timelineScale.ts` (pure coordinate model, header, hatch, default range), `utils/timelineDrag.ts` (pure drag arithmetic, dnd-kit keyboard getter and modifiers), `utils/timelineModel.ts` (the data contract
types and layout constants, `headerHeight()`), `hooks/useTimelinePreview.ts` (drag data + preview context), `components/Timeline.tsx` (the frame; its drop zones are `hooks/useTimelineDropZones.ts`, the pending row-focus wish `hooks/useTimelinePendingFocus.ts`, `revealFocused` `utils/timelineReveal.ts`, the overlay's geometry `utils/timelineGeometry.ts`), `TimelineHeader.tsx`, `TimelineRow.tsx` (+ `TimelineBand`), `TimelineBar.tsx`, `TimelineDndContext.tsx` (its pointer/slot/day readers are `utils/timelineDragTracking.ts`, its announcements `hooks/useTimelineAnnouncements.ts`),
`TimelineToolbar.tsx`, `TimelineUnscheduledTray.tsx`; v0.6 adds `TimelineDrawSurface.tsx` (see "The draw gesture"); v0.4 adds `utils/timelineArrows.ts` and `components/TimelineArrows.tsx` (see "Arrows"); v0.5 adds `utils/timelineLoad.ts` and `components/TimelineLoadStrip.tsx` (see "The load strip"); `theme.module.css` holds the `timeline*` classes; strings in `locales/{en,pl}/timeline.json`.

#### Coordinate model

Dates cross the component boundary as ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` strings; inside they are **epoch days** (whole days since 1970-01-01, UTC; `toEpochDay`/`toIsoDate`). A range is `[from, to]` inclusive. A day is `dayWidth` pixels; a bar spans from the LEFT edge
of its first day to the RIGHT edge of its last: `left = xOf(start)`, `width = (end − start + 1) × dayWidth`. A scale is made by `makeScale(zoom, range)` and carries `xOf(day)`, `widthOf(start, end)`, `dayAt(x)` (floor) and `totalWidth`. Monday = 0 is
`(epochDay + 3) % 7` (`weekdayOf`); the weekend is Saturday–Sunday (`isWeekend`). Public holidays (v0.7) do NOT join that predicate: they come from the dictionary, are a separate hatch layer and a header name (see "Public holidays (built, v0.7.0)"), and `isWeekend` stays the pure Monday-start arithmetic.

| Zoom | px / day | Header groups | Header cells | Weekend hatch | Default range padding (snapped outward) | Empty default |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `week` | 28 | ISO weeks (`W38 2026`) | days (`16`; weekend cells tinted) | yes | ±2 weeks → whole Monday–Sunday weeks | today ±6 weeks |
| `month` | 8 | months (`2026-09`) | ISO weeks (`W38`) | yes | ±1 month → whole months | today ±3 months |
| `quarter` | 3 | quarters (`2026-Q3`) | months (`2026-09`) | no (3 px/day is noise) | ±1 quarter → whole quarters | today ±2 quarters |
| `year` (v0.6) | 1 | years (`2026`) | quarters (`2026-Q3`) | no | ±1 year → whole calendar years | today ±1 year |

**Which zooms a page offers** is its own list: `MILESTONE_ZOOMS` (`week`, `month`, `quarter` — the project and team pages, the `Timeline`'s default `zooms`) and `PLAN_ZOOMS` (`month`, `quarter`, `year` — the roadmap and programs pages, v0.6); `ZOOMS` holds all four. The toolbar renders exactly the `zooms` prop, and a page validates its stored zoom against its own list (`isOneOf(MILESTONE_ZOOMS)`), so a `year` stored by a plan page never reaches a milestone page. The load strip treats `year` like `quarter` (type-complete; the strip is never shown there).
Labels are language-neutral (`formatDate.ts` rule). Groups and cells each PARTITION the range and are clipped to it (a week or month crossing the range edge is a shorter cell, never dropped); a cell may straddle two groups (a week across a month boundary).
`defaultRange(dates, today, zoom)` is the range a page opens with: min/max of the dates ∪ today, padded and snapped as above. **A date the page does not own never stretches it unbounded (1.3):** the Jira dates are external input (the server accepts any date from 1900 to 2999, so a mistyped 2206 would otherwise give every user a track of hundreds of thousands of pixels, and the grow-only range could not be undone by the toggle), so they join the range only inside the Jira window (`jiraWindow`, "The Jira markers (1.3)"). **The page owns the range and keeps it stable across edits** (compute it from the first load, only ever grow it):
a range recomputed from every drag would shift every bar under the pointer. `scrollLeftToCenter` backs the toolbar's Today.

#### Data contract (`utils/timelineModel.ts`)

- `TimelineColumn{id, header, width}` — the sticky left columns (the first is the row header: the name). `leftWidth` = their sum.
- `TimelineRowData{id, cells, bars, bands?, track?}` — `cells[i]` belongs to `columns[i]`: static content (the cell itself is the roving stop) or a **function `(stop) => node`** that spreads `stop` (`data-grid-pos`, `tabIndex`) on its own focusable (a link, the row
  menu button) so one cell never holds two tab stops. `bars` are the row's bars (usually one). `bands` are confined to this row's track. `track` REPLACES the bar rendering of the row's track (positioned with the scale); the row's hatch and bands still draw. A track-only row (no `bars`) exposes no roving columns beyond its left cells, so focusable content inside a `track` brings its own focus handling.
  **No page uses `track`**: the v0.5 load strip is a header band (Q7: a sticky strip), see "The load strip"; the slot stays for a per-row decoration.
- `TimelineBarData{id, start, end, label, describe, editable, tone?}` — `describe` is the bar's accessible name (the page builds it: "{name}, {team}, {start} to {end}, {estimate} man-days"); the component appends ", read-only" (`timeline.bar.readOnly`) when
  `editable` is false. `label` is the short name used by the handles and announcements. Bar ids are unique across the timeline. `tone?: "ghost" | "summary" | "window" | "proposal" | "baseline" | "jira"`: `ghost`/`summary` (v0.4), `proposal` (v0.8), `baseline` (v0.9) and `jira` (1.3) style a READ-ONLY bar (an editable bar ignores them; see "Arrows", "The proposal preview (v0.8.0)" and "The baseline overlay (v0.9.0)"), `window` (v0.6) is honoured on a read-only AND on an editable bar (see "Bar tones").
- `TimelineRowData.draw?: {barId, label}` (v0.6) opts a row in to the draw gesture (see "The draw gesture").
- `TimelineBandData{id, start, end, label, tone: "window" | "drift" | "idle"}` — decorative (`aria-hidden`, `title` = label, `data-band-tone` = the tone); the facts live in the page's text. Passed as the `bands` prop it spans every row (the project's target window, the idle gaps); in a row it spans that row.
  `window` is a quiet gray tint with dashed edges, `drift` is orange (red is never used: nothing blocks in v0.3), `idle` (v0.7) is a diagonal gray hatch with no edges (see "Idle bands (built, v0.7.0)").
- `Timeline` props: `label`, `scale`, `onZoomChange`, `today` (ISO, injected), `columns`, `rows`, `bands?`, `overlay?`, `strip?` (v0.5), `holidays?` (v0.7, "Public holidays"), `toolbar?` (v0.7, the page's own controls after Today), `virtualizeFrom?` (80), `maxHeight?` (640), `busy?` (`aria-busy` on the grid while a save is in flight), `zooms?` (v0.6, default `MILESTONE_ZOOMS`), `ref?: Ref<TimelineHandle>` (v0.6, `focusRow`; a plain prop in React 19, no `forwardRef`).

#### Overlay geometry

`overlay?: (g: TimelineGeometry) => ReactNode` renders its result inside ONE absolutely positioned `<svg aria-hidden>` over the body (`pointer-events: none`, origin = left of the range / top of the first row). `TimelineGeometry` is computed from DATA, never measured:
`scale`, `xOf(iso)`, `rowTop(i) = i × 36`, `rowHeight = 36`, `rowIndexOf(rowId) → index | null` (the row with that `TimelineRowData.id`; the id map is built on first call), `barRect(rowIndex, barId) → {x, y, width, height} | null` (`y = rowTop + 6`, `height = 24`; the committed dates, not a drag preview) and `visibleRows{first, last}` (the mounted
window; draw arrows only for these). Arrows therefore work identically on the plain and the virtual path.

#### Arrows (built, v0.4.0 — the component, wired by the project view)

Files: `utils/timelineArrows.ts` (pure routing + resolution), `components/TimelineArrows.tsx` (the renderer), `TimelineArrowData` in `utils/timelineModel.ts` (the input). A page passes `overlay={(g) => <TimelineArrows arrows={specs} geometry={g} />}`; the component draws, it decides nothing.

**The input contract** — `TimelineArrowData{id, from, to, violated, label}`, `from` the predecessor and `to` the successor, each end `{row, bar, kind}`: `row` = the `TimelineRowData.id` of the row, `bar` = the `TimelineBarData.id` inside it, `kind` = `START` (the bar's left edge) or `END` (its right edge).
Dependency types map as FS = END→START, SS = START→START, FF = END→END, SF = START→END. `violated` draws the arrow red; `label` ("A → B, FS") is the path's `<title>` only. `id` is unique among the arrows. A page maps its view onto this; the component never learns what a dependency is.

**The route** (`arrowRoute`, all legs horizontal or vertical): the arrow leaves its anchor (the middle of the bar's edge) outward, runs vertically to the successor's row and enters the successor's anchor moving INTO the bar. Stubs are 8 px.
- Edges facing the same way (SS, FF) on different rows: one vertical leg just outside the further of the two 8 px stubs; never a backtrack.
- Opposite ways (FS, SF) and the successor's anchor is AHEAD of the predecessor's in the leaving direction (FS: `to.x >= from.x`; SF: `to.x <= from.x`): one vertical leg at `min(8 px, gap / 2)` from the predecessor's edge, then straight in. A narrow gap shrinks the stub instead of reading as a backtrack: back-to-back bars (gap 0) run straight down the shared edge,
  Friday to Monday at quarter zoom (9 px) turns at 4.5 px.
- The BACKTRACK is used only when the successor's anchor is BEHIND the predecessor's in the leaving direction (FS with the successor starting before the predecessor ends — a violation or a lead; SF whenever the successor ends right of the predecessor's start, its usual case) and for SS/FF on the SAME row (their straight leg would run through the bars' centre line;
  not a planned shape, but guarded): the vertical leg runs along the ROW BOUNDARY next to the successor's row (`rowTop(toRow)` when the predecessor's row is above, `rowTop(toRow) + rowHeight` when it is below or the same), a second horizontal leg goes to the approach point (anchor ± 8 px) and a short vertical leg drops onto the row — so the arrow never crosses a bar it connects.
- The overlay svg spans exactly the track, so a stub left of the range start (x < 0) is clipped. That strip is the sticky left columns' (opaque, z-index 3 over the overlay's 2), which would cover it anyway; the page's range is padded outward from its data, so a bar does not touch the range ends in practice.

**Which arrows are drawn** (`arrowsToDraw`): an arrow whose row or bar is unknown to the geometry is skipped; so is an arrow with NEITHER end in `visibleRows` (the mounted window — on the plain path every row, on the virtual path the window with its overscan). An arrow with one mounted end is drawn whole: the other end is positioned from data. An arrow whose both ends lie outside the window but whose
line would cross it is NOT drawn (accepted: the project view never virtualises). **Committed dates only**: the geometry is built from `rows`, so a drag or resize preview does not drag an arrow; the optimistic update moves it at the drop.

**Output** — one `<path data-arrow={id} data-violated={true|false}>` per arrow with a `<title>` and a `marker-end` arrowhead; two `<marker>`s with the FIXED ids `timeline-arrowhead` / `timeline-arrowhead-violated` (one timeline per page). Tests and e2e count `data-arrow` and read `data-violated`; colour is never asserted. **Decorative**: the svg is `aria-hidden`, the accessible surface is the page's dependency table.
Colour: tokens on `timelineGrid` — `--timeline-arrow` (the SOLID dimmed text colour, gray; the path carries `opacity: 0.7` so its line and arrowhead fade as one group instead of compounding an alpha colour where they meet; the head's tip sits on the anchor, `refX = 10`) and `--timeline-violation` (`light-dark(red-8, red-5)`: red = a violated constraint), 1.5 px strokes.

**Bar tones** (`TimelineBarData.tone`, read-only bars only; rendered as a class and a `data-tone` attribute): `ghost` = an endpoint that lives on another project — the bar colour at 35 % with a dashed outline; `summary` = the project's own derived span — hollow, a 2 px outline; `proposal` (v0.8) = a proposed span, hollow with a DASHED 2 px text-colour outline ("The proposal preview (v0.8.0)"); `baseline` (v0.9) = the span a baseline froze, a thin SHADOW bar along the bottom of the row ("The baseline overlay (v0.9.0)"). A tone changes no accessible name: the page's `describe` carries the words
(`timeline.bar.ghost` "{label}, from project {project}" and `timeline.bar.summary` "{name}, the project's span {start} to {end}" are the strings the project view's ghost and summary rows use, `timeline.bar.window` "{name}, target window {start} to {end}" the one the roadmap's window bar uses; its bar names for a link's own ends gain ", N conflicts" while violated links end on them).

**The window tone (v0.6)** — `tone: "window"` is the target window drawn as a BAR, so it can be moved and resized with the bar machinery (the roadmap and programs pages put it in the project's row beside the span bar, `window:<projectId>`). It is the one tone an EDITABLE bar honours: the band look (the `--timeline-band` tint shared with `TimelineBand`, 2 px dashed `--timeline-window-edge` edges, square corners) over the WHOLE row height (the wrapper's `timelineBarFrameWindow` class: `top: 0; bottom: 0`, where an ordinary bar sits 6 px inset), with the edge handles and the keyboard path of any editable bar (`data-tone="window"` on the body, read-only or editable). Held, it turns a translucent accent instead of the solid fill so the bar behind it stays readable. It is mounted before the span bar, so the span paints over it. **A read-only `summary` bar is transparent to the pointer** (v0.6, C6: its frame gets `.timelineBarInert`, `pointer-events: none`; it stays a labelled, focusable image for the keyboard and assistive technology): a project's span usually covers most of its window, so a span that took the pointer would leave the window under it ungrabbable and the row's draw surface unreachable across it. The decision is by tone, for every read-only summary bar on every page (an editable bar ignores the tone, ghost and window bars are unaffected); `Timeline.test.tsx` pins the class, the e2e (`roadmap.spec.ts`, third test) presses the middle of the span bar to move the window and to draw. The accessible name is the page's `describe` (`timeline.bar.window`); arrows never end on a window bar (the pages' arrow specs name the span bars).

**The project view's use** (`components/ProjectTimeline.tsx` composing the row builders of `ProjectTimelineRows.tsx`, `utils/projectDependencies.ts`): rows are the summary row (`project:<id>`, only while the project itself is a link end), the scheduled milestones, then one ghost row per distinct external end with dates (`ghost:m:<id>` / `ghost:p:<id>`, sorted by start, end, name; its name a link to that project). An arrow per link whose status is not UNSCHEDULED, `violated` from the SERVER's check; an end with no row (a tray milestone, a project with no span) is skipped by `arrowsToDraw`. The page's range includes every end's dates.

**Cost.** Resolving the arrows is O(arrows) per render. One-off, NON-BINDING measurement (not a budget; re-measure rather than quote it) with the C4 component, 50 rows and 200 arrows (all drawn), one run on an Apple-silicon laptop (arm64, Node 26, vitest + happy-dom): `arrowsToDraw` median 0.24 ms (p95 0.33 ms) over 200 repetitions; mounting the whole Timeline in happy-dom (DOM construction only: NO layout, NO paint)
15.8 ms median without arrows, 21.3 ms with the 200 arrows (+5 ms; three runs: 20.8–21.3). That is not a browser figure; the real-browser one is the next paragraph. The virtual pages (the v0.5 team page up to ~270 rows, the v0.6 roadmap ~100 and expanded programs tree ~125) re-run the filter on every scroll frame (a few hundred to a few thousand comparisons; measured in the browser with the arrows on at the 60 Hz cap, "Measured figures on the roadmap and programs pages at scale"); a per-row index is the remedy when that is not enough.

**Measured in the project view (C5; one-off, NON-BINDING, this machine only — re-measure rather than quote).** ONE project with 50 scheduled milestones (weekly, 5 days) and 200 links from 20 milestones of a second project (the ghost endpoints; FS/SS/FF/SF in turn, lag 0; 77 of them violated), a throwaway API script, opened as the seed administrator in headless Chromium (1440 x 900, week zoom, the compose stack's production bundle, an Apple-silicon laptop), three fresh page loads:

| What | Figure |

So the O(arrows) resolution lives only in the render (a view change, a zoom), never in a scroll frame, on a project-view-sized page. At 4x zoom the `opacity: 0.7` on a gray path dims its line and its arrowhead as ONE group: head and line read as the same tone (verified in a crop); where two arrows end on the same anchor (an SS and an FS into one bar's start) their heads overlap and read darker, which is the only visible compounding.

#### The load strip (built, v0.5.0 — the slot, wired by the team page)

`strip?: TimelineStripData{label, render(scale)}` renders a THIRD header row (`STRIP_HEIGHT` 36 px) under the date cells: a sticky left caption cell (`strip.label`, visible text) and a track part (`scale.totalWidth` wide, `overflow: hidden`) holding `render(scale)`. The whole row is `aria-hidden` and decorative; the page's weekly table is the accessible surface.
The row is part of the sticky header (z 4), so it stays under the dates while the body scrolls, and scrolls horizontally with the track.

**The header height rule.** The header is `headerHeight(hasStrip) = HEADER_HEIGHT (56) + (hasStrip ? STRIP_HEIGHT : 0)`, computed ONCE in `Timeline.tsx`; every consumer follows it: the drop zone's `visibleBounds().top`, the virtualiser's `scrollMargin` and `scrollPaddingStart`, `revealFocused`'s `clearTop`, and the two CSS literals that were `scroll-margin-top: 56px` (`.timelineCell`, `.timelineBarBody`), now `var(--timeline-head)` — set on the grid's inline style beside `--timeline-left`. With no strip the variable is `56px` and nothing else changes (pinned by `Timeline.test.tsx`). The geometry's origin (the top of the first body row) is unchanged, so arrows need nothing.
Any future sticky header part adds to `headerHeight()`; a literal is a bug.

**The cells** (`components/TimelineLoadStrip.tsx`, geometry in the pure `utils/timelineLoad.ts` `stripCells(weeks, scale)`): input is a LOCAL `LoadWeek` type mirroring the server's week row `{weekStart, load, capacity, overloaded, overloadMd, maxWip, wipExceeded}` (the page maps its API type onto it). One cell per ISO week at `scale.xOf(weekStart)`, seven days wide, clipped to the range like the header's cells (a week wholly outside is dropped; a cut week is a narrow cell of 3-28 px, so the cell is `overflow: hidden` and a cell under 40 px prints no figure — the `title` keeps it); `data-week`, `data-overloaded`, `data-wip-exceeded` and a `title` (the full sentence, `timeline.strip.*`).
- **The column** rises from the baseline; its height is `min(load / capacity, 1.25) x 24 px`; a hairline at 24 px across the cell is the capacity line, so a column past it IS the overload. Gray (`--timeline-bar`), ORANGE (`--timeline-overload` = `light-dark(orange-8, orange-5)`, the drift token's pair) when `overloaded`; a translucent fill under a crisp 2 px top edge, so the figure on top stays readable. A zero capacity (on hold) with a load is the 125 % cap; any non-zero load shows at least a 1 px column. The capacity hairline is the solid dimmed ink (no opacity: >= 3:1 non-text in both schemes), the only "100 %" cue.
- **No capacity** (`capacity: null`: the weeks before the team's first period): no column and no line, only the load figure in the dimmed ink, never flagged — distinct from a capacity of 0.
- **The figure:** week zoom "8.5 / 14", month zoom "8.5", quarter zoom none (a 21 px cell); a prorated capacity is simply the week's number.
- **The WIP mark** (distinct from the overload by SHAPE, the same warning hue): while `wipExceeded`, a small chip with a DASHED orange outline in the cell's top-right corner carrying the peak `maxWip` (week and month zoom), a 6 px dot at quarter zoom. The chip's ink is the text colour (orange text on white is under 4.5:1).
- Tests read the data attributes and the `data-part` of each mark (`capacity-line`, `fill`, `figure`, `wip`), never colour. Pure geometry: `utils/timelineLoad.test.ts`.

**Its first consumer (the team page, `components/TeamTimeline.tsx`):** the page passes `strip={{label, render: (scale) => <TimelineLoadStrip weeks={view.weeks} scale={scale} />}}` with the server's week rows as they are (the API's `WeekLoad` is structurally `LoadWeek`). The weekly table (`TeamWeeklyLoad`) is the accessible surface; its legend is the strip's. Real-browser figures for the team page at scale are recorded here once the sample dataset exists (C6).

#### Drag, resize and keyboard contract

ONE `TimelineDndContext` per page wraps the `Timeline` and the `TimelineUnscheduledTray` (`scale`, `onSchedule(barId, start, end) → boolean`). Sensors: `PointerSensor` (activation distance 5 px) and `KeyboardSensor` with `keyboardCoordinates(dayWidth)` (`scrollBehavior: "auto"`).
Collision is `pointerWithin` (over a filtered candidate list — see "Row drag"). Every draggable carries `DragData{kind, barId, label, start?, end?, position?, count?}`; `kind` is `move | resize-start | resize-end | tray | row | draw` (the fourth family, "Row drag" below, proposes a slot, not dates; the fifth, "The draw gesture" below, proposes a span from two days) and the pure `dropOutcome(kind, bar, deltaDays, dayAtPointer)` turns it into dates:

- `move`: both ends + Δ days. `resize-start`: start + Δ, clamped to ≤ end. `resize-end`: end + Δ, clamped to ≥ start (a one-day bar is the minimum). `tray`: start = the day under the pointer, end = start + 6 (one calendar week; the page's Schedule
  button uses the same `dropOutcome("tray", …)` with its own day). Δ = `round(delta.x / dayWidth)`; bar gestures are held to one row and whole days by the modifiers `horizontalOnly` and `snapToDays(dayWidth)` (a tray item is left free).
- The tray's day is `dayAt(pointer.x − zoneLeft)` while `over` is the track drop zone (`TIMELINE_DROP_ID`, an invisible element exactly as wide as the range and as tall as every row) AND the pointer is inside the part of it the scroller shows: the zone's droppable `data` carries two live readers, `visibleBounds()` (the scroller's rectangle minus the sticky columns on the left and the sticky header on top) and `zoneLeft()` (the zone element's `getBoundingClientRect().left`), both read at the moment of the event; a release over the tray, the footer or the left columns schedules nothing, and neither does the keyboard (no coordinates). **The pointer is the live one**: `TimelineDndContext` records it from the activator on and follows `pointermove` on `window` for the length of a tray drag. dnd-kit's own `delta` is NOT used for a tray item: once `over` is the zone, dnd-kit takes the scroll offsets of the OVER node's scrollers, so `delta` gains the grid's whole `scrollLeft` (a drop landed thousands of pixels away — found by the e2e), and `over.rect` is a snapshot from the drag's start that any later scroll makes stale. One timeline per page: `TIMELINE_DROP_ID` and the DndContext id are fixed.
- Bar anatomy: a `div` wrapper positioned by the scale holding three SIBLING draggables (no button inside a button): the body (translated in place while moved) and the two edge handles, which sit OUTSIDE the bar (`left/right: −w`) with a CONSTANT width per zoom (6 px at week/month, 4 px at quarter) — a width derived from the live bar would change mid-resize and make dnd-kit re-measure the node it is dragging, and outside handles leave even a 1-day bar at quarter zoom (3 px) its whole body as the move target (a pointer hit on 3 px is still precise work: the keyboard path is the reliable one there). While an EDGE is held, the bar redraws from the context's
  preview dates (`useTimelinePreview`); a move keeps its geometry and translates. Read-only bars are a labelled `role="img"`, no handles.
- **Autoscroll** is dnd-kit's, narrowed per gesture: its default zone is 20 % of the scroller on both axes, which a bar near the right edge of the grid sits inside — a one-day resize there scrolled the grid by days under a 28 px pointer move (found by the e2e). A BAR gesture gets `{ x: 0.05, y: 0.02 }` (along time only, and only when the held bar really reaches the edge; never zero, dnd-kit divides by the threshold); a TRAY drag gets `{ x: 0.05, y: 0.15 }` (it travels from the tray under the grid up to it, so the page or a tall grid may need to scroll vertically). `autoScroll` is a prop of the one `DndContext`, so the context swaps the two configs at drag start and back at the end. Trade-off: dnd-kit measures the zone on the scrollers of the node the item is OVER, so a tray item held over the grid also scrolls the grid's rows near its top/bottom edge — wanted with 1,000 rows, a little eager with 20.
- `onDragEnd` proposes through `onSchedule` only when the outcome differs from the bar's current dates; a cancel, a no-op or a tray drop outside the track proposes nothing. `onSchedule` is a proposal and answers whether the page TOOK it: a refusal (`false` — the project view refuses while a save is running) is never silent — dnd-kit's drop announcement is corrected to `timeline.announce.dropRefused` ("… was not changed: a save is still running"), the bar snaps back, and the page shows and announces its own "try again" notice (the Schedule button and the schedule dialog get the same treatment through the page's one `commit`).
- **Keyboard:** Space or Enter on a focused bar or handle picks it up; ←/→ one day, Shift+←/→ seven; Space/Enter drops (`onSchedule`); Escape cancels; ↑/↓ do nothing while held. A resize cannot cross the opposite edge: the getter clamps the displacement from the held bar's dates and steps from the clamped value, so ← after an overshoot is never silent. Read-only bars are not touch drag targets (the page still scrolls under a finger). While a bar is held the grid's own arrow handling is suspended (`useDndContext().active`).
- **Announcements** are dnd-kit's own live region, localized (`timeline.announce.*`, EN/PL): pick-up (kind-specific, with the dates and the keys), every step (`name: start to end`, said once per distinct span), the drop, cancel; for a tray item: pick-up, scheduled/not scheduled, cancel. The
  `screenReaderInstructions` (`timeline.dnd.instructions`) are the `aria-describedby` text of every draggable. A page announces its own non-drag changes (the Schedule button, the schedule dialog, the save verdict) in its polite live region.
- **The deterministic path** (the Roadmap rule: a dnd-kit surface always ships explicit controls): the page provides "Edit schedule…" (a dialog with two date inputs and Unschedule) and the tray's Schedule button. The tray's drag handle is pointer-only (`aria-hidden`, no keyboard
  sensor attached); the Schedule button is its keyboard and screen-reader path. The tray is deliberately not in a scroll region: a dragged row translates out of the table over the timeline and a scroller would clip it.

#### Row drag (built, v0.5.0 — the team page's order)

A team manager reorders the page's rows by dragging a row's handle. It is the **fourth drag kind (`kind: "row"`) on the ONE `TimelineDndContext`**, not a second context: dnd-kit's hooks bind to the NEAREST `DndContext`, so a sortable context inside the Timeline would capture every bar's `useDraggable`, and a sibling cannot exist (headers and bars live in the same row elements). `@dnd-kit/sortable` is not used either — it translates every sibling row, and a transform on a row makes a stacking context per row (the reason virtual rows use `top`): the sticky left cells (z 3) would drop under the overlay svg (z 2), and rows that are not mounted could not shift anyway. **No row ever gets a transform**: the held row stays put, a `DragOverlay` card follows the pointer and an insertion line marks the slot.

- **The handle** (`components/TimelineRowHandle.tsx`): `useDraggable({id: "row:<id>", data: {kind: "row", barId, label, position, count}})` with ONLY `listeners.onPointerDown` attached (the tray handle's rule: never the keyboard sensor's `onKeyDown`, no `attributes`), `aria-hidden`, not focusable, `touch-action: none`. The row's header cell stays a STATIC roving stop, so "one cell, one stop" holds; a pointer press on the handle focuses the cell (the row becomes the tab stop) without a reveal (`revealFocused` acts on `:focus-visible` only). The page renders it first in the name cell, only with the view's `can.editOrder`. **The keyboard and screen-reader path is the row menu** (Move up / down / to top / to bottom) — the Roadmap rule, with the handle pointer-only like the tray's.
- **The row zone** (`TIMELINE_ROW_DROP_ID`): a second invisible droppable over the WHOLE body (`leftWidth + totalWidth` wide, `bodyHeight` tall, `pointer-events: none`, `aria-hidden`), rendered by every `Timeline` and inert unless a row is held. Its `data` carries three live readers, the track zone's idiom: `zoneTop()` (its `getBoundingClientRect().top` read at the event — negative once scrolled), `rowBounds()` (the scroller from its left edge, below `headerHeight(hasStrip)`) and `rowCount` (`rowBounds()` is `{left, right, top, bottom, scrollerTop}`: `top` is where the rows start, `scrollerTop` the scroller's own top edge). dnd-kit measures droppables once at drag start, so its rectangle of the zone is a snapshot that does not reach over the sticky header at a scroll offset of 0 — a row drag therefore does not use `pointerWithin` on it (see "Collision filter"); every number comes from the live readers.
- **The slot** (`utils/timelineDrag.ts`): `rowSlotAt(y, zoneTop, rowCount) = clamp(round((y − zoneTop) / 36), 0, rowCount)` while the live pointer (tracked from the activator on, like a tray drag's) is over the rows. **Over the sticky header** (inside the scroller horizontally, above `rowBounds().top`) the slot is the first one that lies below the header (`rowSlotWithin`: `ceil((top − zoneTop) / 36)`), not `null`: the header is most of the scroller's top-15 % autoscroll zone, and a target that vanished there would make scrolling UP with a held row nearly impossible. Outside the scroller (the tray, the page around the grid) the slot is `null` and nothing lands. The upper half of a row is the slot before it, the lower half the slot after it. The body is `rows × 36` px and row `i` sits at `i × 36` mounted or not, so **a slot between rows that are not mounted is as valid as any other**: the arithmetic never looks for a row element. `rowPositionAfter(index, slot)` is the announced 1-based position; a slot of `index` or `index + 1` is a no-op (`isRowNoop`) — announced as "stays" from the pick-up position, but the context still PROPOSES it: the page judges against the order it shows NOW (a refetch mid-drag can have moved the row).
- **Collision filter**: the row zone lies under the track zone, so the collision detection answers a `row` drag with the row zone ALWAYS (whether the pointer is really over the rows, the header or outside is the slot function's question, answered from the live pointer) and every other kind with `pointerWithin` over everything EXCEPT it — bars and the tray see exactly the droppables they saw before.
- **Preview, indicator, overlay**: `TimelinePreview` is the bar preview or `{kind: "row", barId, label, slot | null}` (set at pick-up with a `null` slot, updated only when the slot changes — one `setPreview` per 36 px). `components/TimelineRowDropIndicator.tsx` (rendered by `Timeline` after the rows; it reads the preview ITSELF, so a bar drag never re-renders the Timeline) draws a 2 px line at `max(slot × 36 − 1, 0)` (`data-row-slot`; z 3, after the rows in the DOM: over the sticky left cells but UNDER the sticky header, so a slot scrolled up beneath the header is covered by it instead of painting across it). The context mounts `<DragOverlay modifiers={[verticalOnly]} dropAnimation={null}>` with a `timelineRowGhost` card **only while a row is held** (the preview is a row's). **A mounted `DragOverlay` is not inert for other drags**: dnd-kit renders its fixed, z-index 999 wrapper for EVERY drag while one is mounted, even with no children, and then runs the drag in "overlay mode" (`usesDragOverlay`: no node-rect delta, no scroll compensation of the dragged node) — a bar dragged while the grid autoscrolls horizontally would drift from the pointer and an invisible wrapper would sit over it. So it is mounted for a row drag only; the bars' keyboard getter and translation are unchanged (the unit test runs the REAL overlay under a keyboard-held bar and asserts no wrapper). The context's own modifiers pass a `row` transform through like a tray's. The overlay is `position: fixed`, so no scroller clips it.
- **Autoscroll**: `ROW_AUTO_SCROLL = {threshold: {x: 0.02, y: 0.15}}`, swapped in at pick-up like the tray's. dnd-kit scrolls the scrollable ancestors of the OVER node (else the active one) — both inside the grid's scroll region — so holding a row near the top or bottom 15 % scrolls it, rows mount as they come into the window, and the slot follows the pointer against the live `zoneTop()`.
- **A row that unmounts mid-drag**: dnd-kit swaps an active node's `data` for an empty object when its draggable unmounts, which a virtualised row's handle does when autoscroll carries the row out of the window. The context therefore keeps the picked-up `DragData` (`held`) and resolves the drag from it when the active data is empty; the drop still knows what was held.
- **The drop**: slot `null` → nothing proposed, announced `dropRowNone`; otherwise `onReorder(barId, slot) → boolean` for every non-null slot (the page decides: `placed` answers `true` WITHOUT a PUT when the permutation equals the displayed order, so a drop beside the row sends nothing) (a new optional prop, `onSchedule`'s twin; the page's is `order.dropAt` — optimistic `applyTeamOrder`, the PUT against `order.revision`, the stale 409 → rollback and reload). `false` (a save in flight) is announced as `dropRefused` and the page shows its "try again" note. Pages without reordering (the project view) render no handle and pass no `onReorder`.
- **Announcements** (dnd-kit's region, `timeline.announce.*` EN/PL): `startRow` "Picked up {name}, position {n} of {count}. Move the pointer to the new place and release; Escape cancels.", `stepRow` (once per distinct slot), `dropRow` "Dropped {name} at position {n} of {count}.", `dropRowNone`, `cancelRow`. `screenReaderInstructions` ride on elements that spread `attributes`; the handle does not, so no bar-key instructions are read on it.
- **Tests**: the slot arithmetic and `moveToSlot` are table-tested; `Timeline.test.tsx` feeds the wrapped context the row events (overlay, indicator, announcements, `onReorder`, the refusal, the lost-data case, the collision filter); `TeamDetails.test.tsx` pins the PUT; the real pointer drag is one e2e scenario (`team-view.spec.ts`, with Escape) — a drag through autoscroll to unmounted rows is a manual pass on the sample plan.

#### The draw gesture (built, v0.6.0 — the fifth drag kind)

A row with no target window yet opts in with `TimelineRowData.draw = {barId, label}` (`barId` = the id the window bar will have, `window:<projectId>`): a pointer drag across its EMPTY stretch draws the window. It is the **fifth kind (`kind: "draw"`) on the ONE `TimelineDndContext`**, for the same reason as the row kind (dnd-kit binds to the nearest context).

- **The surface** (`components/TimelineDrawSurface.tsx`, mounted by `TimelineRow` FIRST in the track, behind the bands and bars, so a bar or a band always wins the pointer where it sits): an absolutely positioned `aria-hidden` div, `useDraggable({id: "draw:<barId>", data: {kind: "draw", barId, label, trackLeft}})` with ONLY `listeners.onPointerDown` attached (the row handle's rule: never the keyboard sensor's `onKeyDown`, no `attributes`, not focusable, no roving stop of its own). `touch-action: pan-x pan-y pinch-zoom`: a finger scrolls the grid instead of drawing, so **the page's dialog is the touch, keyboard and screen-reader path** (the Roadmap rule). While a draw runs it paints a ghost over the span so far (`[data-draw-preview]`, the window look: `.timelineDrawGhost` shares `.timelineBarWindow`'s declarations).
- **The span** (`utils/timelineDrag.ts` `drawOutcome(anchorDay, pointerDay)`): `[min, max]` of the two epoch days, either direction, one day when equal (the minimum). The ANCHOR is the day under the pointer-down, read at pick-up from the activator's `clientX` against the surface's own live left edge (`trackLeft`: a drag has no `over` yet when it starts) and then fixed as an epoch day (the grid may autoscroll under the held pointer). The POINTER day is `scale.dayAt(pointer.x − zoneLeft())` with the TRACK zone's live reader and the live pointer tracked from the activator on, exactly the tray's rule; **the pointer's day is clamped to `[scale.from, scale.to]` (dnd-kit's zone rect is a snapshot, so after an autoscroll the pointer can sit over the sticky columns with a negative offset — about 300 days before the range at Year zoom), and while the pointer is not over the track zone (the tray, the page around the grid) the last span is kept**, and a drop there still proposes it. A draw does NOT use `visibleBounds`: drawing leftwards under the sticky columns must keep working so the autoscroll can bring the days into view. A pick-up with no pointer position (a non-pointer activator) has no anchor: nothing is previewed, nothing is proposed.
- **Collision, modifiers, autoscroll**: the collision is the bars' (`pointerWithin` minus the row zone); the modifier passes a `draw` transform through like a tray's; the autoscroll stays the bars' `{x: 0.05, y: 0.02}` (along time only; no swap at pick-up). Year zoom is 1 px per day: a 5 px activation is five days of anchor error if read late, which is why the anchor comes from the pointer-DOWN.
- **The drop** is `onSchedule(barId, start, end)` — the SAME seam as a bar move, with the `barId` the row would have, so a page handles move, resize and draw in one `window:<id>` branch; `false` (a save in flight) is announced as `dropRefused` and the page shows its "try again" note. A cancel proposes nothing and removes the ghost and the pointer listener.
- **Announcements** (dnd-kit's region, `timeline.announce.*` EN/PL): `startDraw` "Drawing {name}. Move the pointer across the days and release; Escape cancels.", the existing `step` ("{name}: {start} to {end}", once per distinct span), `dropDraw` "Drew {name}: {start} to {end}.", `cancelDraw`.
- **Tests**: `drawOutcome` is table-tested (`timelineDrag.test.ts`); `Timeline.test.tsx` "the draw gesture" feeds the wrapped context (the surface mounts only with `draw`, both directions, the live zone left, the kept span, no anchor, cancel, refusal, announcements, the listener, the collision); the real pointer draw is the third scenario of `e2e/tests/roadmap.spec.ts` (the roadmap and programs pages use it: `projectTrack` sets `draw` for a project with no window whose `can.editTargetWindow` is set).

#### Accessibility model

A `div role="grid"` (labelled, `aria-rowcount`, per-row `aria-rowindex`; the header's cell row is row 1, body row `i` is `i + 1`) with `role="row"` rows of `rowheader`/`gridcell`s, inside the labelled, focusable `ScrollRegion` (`scrollRef` is its one extension). Not a listbox: a listbox
option may not contain interactive children (bars, handles, menus). Not a `<table>`: virtual rows are absolutely positioned, which a table row cannot be. The header's group row and date cells are decoration (`aria-hidden`); the bars' dates live in their accessible names.

**`focusRow(rowId)` (v0.6)** — `Timeline` accepts `ref?: Ref<TimelineHandle>` and exposes `focusRow(rowId)` through `useImperativeHandle` (the roadmap's flag panel "Show in timeline"). A MOUNTED row: its row-header cell (`[data-grid-pos="r,0"]`) takes the focus and is revealed with `revealFocused(target, force = true)` — `revealFocused` acts on `:focus-visible` only, and a programmatic focus after a click is not focus-visible, so `force` skips that test (the same arithmetic, clear of the sticky header). An UNMOUNTED row (the virtual path): `pendingFocus = {r, c: 0}` + `virtualizer.scrollToIndex`, and the existing layout effect focuses the cell as soon as the row mounts (`scrollToIndex` already lands it below the header); the wish is dropped by the same events as an arrow key's. An unknown id does nothing.

**One roving tab stop** (`hooks/useRovingGrid.ts`, now generic over the grid element): ↑/↓ rows, ←/→ cells, Home/End row ends, Ctrl+Home/End grid ends. Columns of a row: the left cells `0…L−1`, then per bar `L + 3b` = the bar, `+1` its start handle, `+2` its end handle (editable only).
Rows have different widths, so a VERTICAL move lands on the target row's nearest lower column when the exact one is absent (a read-only row has no handle cells) and Ctrl+End goes to the last cell that exists on the last row. `isValid` treats an unmounted row or an absent column as invalid, so the
tab stop falls back to the first mounted row. Cells carry `scroll-margin` (`var(--timeline-head)`, sticky columns) so a focused cell scrolls clear of the sticky parts when the browser decides to scroll — and it does not when the item is only PARTLY covered by them (it does not count sticky elements as covering), so the grid also reveals the focused item itself (`revealFocused` on the grid's `focus` event, only for `:focus-visible` — a mouse press must not move the grid under the pointer: it measures the target against the part of the scroller that is really shown, inside its border so a classic scrollbar is excluded, and scrolls the difference, never further than the item's own START edge reaching the sticky parts, so a bar wider than the visible track shows its start; left cells only vertically). Found by the 1,000-row measurement: a bar half under the columns took focus with its ring hidden.

**VoiceOver reading order of a keyboard bar move (Space, then an arrow, then Space): NOT yet done (noted 2026-11-05).** It needs a person with VoiceOver on the compose stack; the order the announcements arrive in (the grab, the move, the drop; dnd-kit's live region is the one announcer for drags) is to be recorded here as a dated note. The automated parts (axe over the grid, the open row menus and the baseline overlays, both themes) are in `e2e/tests/accessibility.spec.ts`; the roving grid at 100+ rows was probed in 1.0.0 ("Measured figures (1.0.0)").

#### Virtualisation

The body is at least `MIN_BODY_ROWS` (3) rows tall even with fewer rows, so an empty or nearly empty timeline still has an area to drop an Unscheduled item on (the drop zone is as tall as the body). Rows are virtualised with `@tanstack/react-virtual` (`useVirtualizer`; fixed 36 px rows, `overscan` 8, the `ScrollRegion` as the scroller, `scrollMargin` = the header's height, `headerHeight()`: 56 px, 92 with a strip) **only when `rows.length > virtualizeFrom`** (default 80). Both paths mount the same `TimelineRow`; the virtual one gives
each row `position: absolute; top: index × 36` inside a body of `rows × 36` px (`top`, not `transform`: a transform makes a stacking context per row and the sticky left cells could not rise above the today line and overlay). The project view (10–50 rows) never virtualises; the v0.5 team view (up to ~270 rows)
and the v0.6 roadmap (~100 rows) and fully expanded programs tree (~125) do. **↑/↓ and Ctrl+Home/End at an unmounted row** are taken by the Timeline: it `scrollToIndex`es, remembers the wanted cell and focuses it (or the nearest cell to its left) in a layout effect as soon as the row mounts; that wish is dropped by any other key, a focus elsewhere, or a scroll by hand (wheel, touch, pointer, a key on the scroller), so it cannot steal focus later. `scrollToIndex` lands the row below the sticky header (`scrollPaddingStart`). Horizontal (time-axis) virtualisation
is NOT built: a range is bounded by the project's span plus padding, the hatch is one periodic CSS background per row and the today line one element, so the only per-day DOM is the header's day cells (`BACKLOG.md`: the time-axis item; the v0.6 plan pages measured at 1,826-4,872 px of track, "Measured figures on the roadmap and programs pages at scale").

#### Hatching and styling

The weekend hatch is ONE `repeating-linear-gradient(90deg, …)` per row track (`weekendBackground(scale)`): period `7 × dayWidth`, transparent Monday–Friday, the `--timeline-hatch` ink on Saturday–Sunday, phased to the Monday on or before the range start (a repeating gradient repeats both ways, so the phase may be
negative). Drawn at week and month zoom, not at quarter. Since v0.7 a second layer rides on it for public holidays ("Public holidays (built, v0.7.0)" below). Bars are data, so they are neutral gray (`--timeline-bar`); the brand cyan appears only on the focus ring and on the bar being dragged. The today line is the text colour (2 px, 55 % opacity) with a "Today" tag in the header. All styling
is `light-dark()` in `theme.module.css` (`timelineGrid` defines the tokens). Stacking, bottom to top: bands and today line (no z-index; painted first) < row tracks and bars (the line shows through the tracks' transparent parts and passes UNDER the bars) < the overlay (z 2) < the sticky left cells (z 3) < the sticky header (z 4).

#### Public holidays (built, v0.7.0)

`Timeline` takes `holidays?: readonly {date, name}[]` (the dictionary's rows; any range, the component filters to its own) — **a page passes `useHolidays()`'s list straight through; all four timeline components do** (`ProjectTimeline`, `TeamTimeline`, `RoadmapTimeline`, `ProgramsTimeline`), the plan pages included (the tint shows at their Month zoom). The component draws; the server's views are what decide any figure. Pure functions in `utils/timelineScale.ts`, one `holidayDayMap` (epoch day → name) built per render:

- **The row hatch is two layers.** `trackBackground(scale, map)` = `holidayBackground(...)` then `weekendBackground(scale)`, joined with `, ` into the ONE `backgroundImage` string computed once in `Timeline.tsx` and handed to every row as `hatch` (so the row still carries a single inline style). `holidayBackground` is one `linear-gradient(90deg, transparent 0px X0px, INK X0px X1px, transparent X1px …)` over the whole track (the track is `totalWidth` wide, so the stops are plain offsets from the range start; runs of consecutive holiday working days merge into one ink band; the last stop is `transparent X1px`). The SAME `--timeline-hatch` ink as the weekend and the same `HATCHED_ZOOMS` (week, month; none at quarter/year, 3 px a day is noise). **A holiday on a Saturday or Sunday is left out of the gradient** (the translucent ink would compound with the weekend stripe); it is still listed in the accessible paragraph. `null` where neither layer is drawn.
- **The header.** At week zoom the day cell of a holiday gets the weekend tint class and `title="Public holiday: {{name}}"` (`timeline.holidayTitle`) plus `data-holiday="true"` (`HeaderCell.holiday?`, set only on a DAY cell by `headerRows(scale, map)`; week/month/quarter cells and the group row never carry one). The header row is `aria-hidden`, so the title is a pointer affordance only.
- **The accessible text.** A `VisuallyHidden` `<p>` right after the toolbar: "Public holidays shown: 2027-01-06 Święto Trzech Króli, 2027-04-05 …" (`timeline.holidaysInView`, `{{list}}` = `date name` joined by `, `, in date order, the holidays inside the range, weekend ones included). It is rendered only at the zooms that draw a holiday (week, month) and only when at least one is in range, so the plan pages' multi-year Year zoom never prints a 70-entry paragraph. **Capped at `MAX_LISTED_HOLIDAYS` (10):** above that, `holidayListing(shown, today)` makes it "N public holidays shown; the next ones: …" (`timeline.holidaysInViewMany`) naming the first ten on or after `today` (the last ten when the whole view is in the past) — a month-zoom multi-year plan page holds ~40.
- **When the dictionary failed to load** the page passes `holidaysFailed` and `Timeline` shows a small muted line after the toolbar, "Public holidays could not be loaded; days off are not marked." (`timeline.holidaysUnavailable`), so a view whose server figures exclude holidays never silently draws them as normal days. A failed REFETCH keeps the last copy and shows nothing.
- **Not here:** the range never grows for a holiday (only the pages' own dates drive it), no holiday is a bar or a band, no arrow or figure is computed from the dictionary on the client — the one client rule is the schedule dialog's "at least one working day" pre-check (`hasWorkingDay`, `web-internals.md` "Project view").

#### Idle bands (built, v0.7.0)

#### The Jira markers (1.3)

The four timeline views carry, per row, what an external tracker says about it; the shared Timeline draws it as a dashed outline bar and the pages as flags (the web half: C8/C9, "Deviations, bindings and the Jira chips" in `web-internals.md`). **The server half** is a document contract and nothing is drawn from the server's side: `jira: {bindingId, connectionId, issueKey, start, end}|null` on a project row (roadmap, programs, `ProjectView.project`) and on a milestone row (project and team views; scheduled and unscheduled alike), `flags[]` (the Jira kinds, `planning-model.md` "Jira flags and links") on the same rows, and `jiraSites[{connectionId, siteUrl}]` ONCE per document so a row never carries a URL (`siteUrl + "/browse/" + issueKey`). `jira`, `flags` and `jiraSites` are always present (`null`/`[]` when there is nothing); **since 1.15 inside a flag only `kind` and `severity` are always present and every other member is ABSENT when it does not apply** (`days`, `teamId`, `otherProjectId`, `deviation`, `count`), a flag's `deviation` is `{kind, detail}` (the severity is the flag's own, not repeated) and a `detail`'s null members (`jira`, `plan`, `days`, `count`) are absent too (the same on the lines of `GET /views/deviations`); `jira.rollup` and the link's dates stay as they are, always present. The reason for the gate is the same as v0.9's: the wire is gzip (the server compresses), the response shape is the one the plan chose, and a smaller document would mean dropping the always-present nulls the contract promises. The project view gate (v0.9: about +2 KB for the overlay) is now <= 40 KB with the overlay for a ten-milestone project (14.3 KB measured). Slimming is the lever and is `BACKLOG.md` "Slim the Jira blocks".

**Latency (the plan's open question).** The Jira computation is the deviations view's own (`DeviationViewService.computeInTransaction`, scoped to the requested nodes) run inside each view's snapshot. Measured on the same rows with the SAME services built without and with the source: the roadmap view **8.9 ms -> 59.2 ms** (1,100 bindings: +50 ms, about 45 us a binding) and the team view **7.7 ms -> 23.4 ms** (301 bindings: +16 ms); the project view's ten bindings are lost in the noise. Where the roadmap's ~50 ms go (one profiled run): the bindings join with its observation columns 15 ms, the nodes (three batched joins) 13 ms, the kernels and item objects 10 ms, the bound projects' derived spans 5.5 ms, the parents' bindings 3 ms; nothing is an N+1. The HTTP p50s above include the harness's fixed cost. The v0.9 roadmap p50 (16.7 ms on the compose stack) will therefore read roughly 60-80 ms on a plan with every project and milestone bound; an unbound plan costs one empty statement (the bindings read; the connections are resolved only for the bindings found). A leaner projection (the fold needs no fingerprint, no `DeviationItem` and no creator names) is `BACKLOG.md` "Slim the Jira blocks" too.

#### Deliberate departures from the bootstrap sketch
